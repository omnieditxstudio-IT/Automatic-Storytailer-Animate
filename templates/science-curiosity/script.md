# The Edge of the Known Universe

Every night, light from billions of distant galaxies travels across cosmic voids.

What seems like empty space is actually brimming with invisible forces.

Compared to our solar system, neighboring star clusters operate on unimaginable scales.

Before advanced deep-space sensors, our view was fundamentally limited.

After orbital telescopes deployed, cosmic horizons expanded dramatically.

# The Mechanics

First, massive stars fuse hydrogen under extreme gravitational compression.

Then, core collapse triggers violent supernova explosions.

Finally, superdense neutron remnants warp the fabric of spacetime.

# By the Numbers

A single teaspoon of neutron star matter weighs over 6 billion tons.

Light travels at approximately 300000 kilometers per second.

The universe does not explain itself. We have to discover it.
